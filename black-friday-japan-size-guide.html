Black Friday Shopping from Japan: How to Get the Size Right

A discount is only a good deal if the item fits. Here is a quick checklist for choosing Japanese sizes before you check out.

Why size matters more in a sale

Sale items are often marked as final sale, with no returns or exchanges. Even when returns are allowed, sending a package back to Japan can cost more than the discount you got. Check the store's return policy for sale items before you order.

Japanese sizes also tend to run smaller than US sizes. A Japanese M is often closer to a US S, so ordering your usual letter size is a common mistake.

5-minute size checklist

  • Open the size chart, not just the size label. Japanese stores usually list garment measurements in centimeters. Use the size chart translator to read terms like 着丈 (length) and 身幅 (body width).
  • Double the flat measurements. 身幅 is measured across one side of the garment. A 身幅 of 55 cm is about 110 cm (43.3 in) around.
  • Compare with something you own. Lay a shirt or pair of pants that fits you well on a flat surface, measure it the same way, and compare the numbers.
  • Check the fit words. オーバーサイズ means oversized, ゆったり means relaxed, and タイト means tight. These tell you how the brand intends the item to fit.
  • For sneakers, convert from centimeters. Japanese shoe sizes are foot length in cm. Use the shoe size converter, and check the brand's own chart if it has one.

Do this before the sale starts, so you can check out quickly when popular sizes start selling out.
