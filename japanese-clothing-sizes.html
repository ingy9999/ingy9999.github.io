Size guide

Japanese clothing sizes vs US sizes

Most Japanese brands base their sizes on body measurements from the Japanese Industrial Standards (JIS). In practice, Japanese sizes usually run about one size smaller than US sizes.

Some brands also offer LL (XL) and larger sizes. Their ranges vary, so check the brand's own chart.

What this means when you shop

  • Don't just order your usual letter size. A Japanese M is often closer to a US S.
  • Watch for numbered sizes. Some Japanese brands use 0, 1, 2 or 1, 2, 3 instead of letters. These do not follow a common standard, so always check the chart.
  • UK and EU stores mostly use similar letter sizes to the US, but numeric EU sizes vary by brand.
  • Fit matters more than the label. An oversized fit can run several sizes larger than the chart suggests.
